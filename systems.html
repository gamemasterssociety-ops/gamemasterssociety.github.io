Every tabletop system creates a different experience. These short guides explain what players generally do, how the rules feel, and what makes each system distinctive.

Current Systems

What to expect at the table

The Game Master and adventure still shape the tone of every table, but these profiles can help you choose a ruleset and style that sound right for you.

D&D

Heroic fantasy

Dungeons & Dragons 5e

Players create adventurers with distinct classes, abilities, and personalities, then work together through exploration, roleplay, problem-solving, and combat. Most uncertain actions use a twenty-sided die, while characters gain new powers as the story continues.

D&D offers many character choices and tends to feel more structured during combat than during conversation or exploration. The setting can change dramatically from one adventure to another, from classic fantasy to magic school stories or highly customized homebrew worlds.

Rules feel
Moderate, with tactical options
Common focus
Adventure, teamwork, roleplay, exploration, and combat
Good fit if you enjoy
Building a capable hero and solving problems as a party

CoC

Investigative horror

Call of Cthulhu

Players take the roles of investigators uncovering mysteries, dangerous secrets, and forces beyond ordinary understanding. Characters are usually capable people rather than powerful heroes, so research, interviews, observation, and cautious decisions often matter more than fighting.

Skills are written as percentages, making your chance of success easy to understand. Physical danger is serious, and encounters with the unnatural can test an investigator’s Sanity. The system is built for suspense, discovery, and consequences.

Rules feel
Moderate and straightforward
Common focus
Investigation, horror, atmosphere, and difficult choices
Good fit if you enjoy
Mysteries where knowledge can be as dangerous as the threat

T2K

Grounded survival

Twilight: 2000 4e

Twilight: 2000 focuses on ordinary people trying to survive after modern society and military order have broken down. Travel, supplies, shelter, injuries, trust, and the people you choose to protect can be as important as any firefight.

The rules support open-ended survival, base building, and detailed small-unit action. Combat can be tense and dangerous, so planning and resource management matter. GMS adventures may adapt the system to a different location or alternate-history conflict while keeping its grounded survival play.

Rules feel
Moderate, with tactical and survival detail
Common focus
Scarcity, travel, community, tactics, and hard choices
Good fit if you enjoy
Consequential survival stories and practical problem-solving

PA

Analog horror mystery

Public Access

Players portray the Deep Lake Latchkeys, people connected to a strange New Mexico town and the public-access television station that vanished from it. The group investigates local mysteries while gradually uncovering personal memories and a larger conspiracy surrounding TV Odyssey.

The rules emphasize conversation, clues, character history, and collaborative interpretation rather than tactical combat. Players help build the town and connect evidence into theories, so the mystery develops through everyone’s ideas. Expect nostalgia, unsettling imagery, humor, and mounting dread.

Rules feel
Light to moderate and narrative-focused
Common focus
Investigation, shared worldbuilding, memory, and analog horror
Good fit if you enjoy
Slow-burn mysteries and helping shape what the clues mean
