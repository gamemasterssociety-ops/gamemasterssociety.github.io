Planning together

Clear responsibilities from the beginning.

Every arrangement is different. These are common responsibilities to discuss before confirming an event.

GMS may help with

  • Event format and audience guidance
  • Connections to appropriate GMS-affiliated Game Masters
  • Beginner-friendly structure
  • Promotional copy and GMS channel support
  • Player expectations and table standards
  • Suggested room layout and registration approach
  • Post-event feedback planning

The organization may provide

  • Appropriate space, tables, chairs and lighting
  • Accessibility, parking and arrival information
  • Venue-specific rules and a planning contact
  • Food or beverage service, when applicable
  • Promotion through organization channels
  • Rental, insurance or agreement requirements
  • A day-of contact for venue questions

The Game Master generally handles

  • Preparing and running the game
  • Table-specific player communication
  • Materials needed for the session
  • Rules instruction during beginner events
  • Direct player questions about the adventure
  • Ticketing and refund expectations when assigned to the GM
  • Any additional responsibilities agreed for that event

Independent Game Masters

GMS is a platform, community, and organizing body. GMS-affiliated Game Masters generally operate independently. Responsibilities, ticketing, payment, venue costs, promotion, and other terms are established for each opportunity.

Common questions

What businesses and organizations usually ask

How much does an event cost?

Pricing depends on the format, number of tables, session length, location, venue costs, preparation requirements, and available Game Masters. GMS will discuss the arrangement before anything is confirmed.

How many people can participate?

A typical table includes one Game Master and approximately four to six players. Larger events may use multiple tables when the space, format, and available Game Masters support it.

Do participants need experience?

No. Beginner events can be designed for people who have never played before. The event description should clearly state whether characters, dice, or other materials are provided.

How much space is needed?

Each table needs comfortable seating, good lighting, moderate noise levels, and enough room for character sheets, dice, notebooks, and any game materials. Most sessions run for approximately three to four hours.

Can you support youth or family events?

Potentially. The age range, parent-facing expectations, venue policies, Game Master availability, content, supervision, and other safeguards must be defined for each program.

Can food or alcohol be present?

That depends on the venue and intended audience. Food, beverage, age restrictions, responsible alcohol service, noise, and table placement should all be discussed during planning.
